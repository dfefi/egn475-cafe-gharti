# Cafe Gharti

## Project choice

I chose the cafe ordering application option because I understand the customer
experience of ordering food and the need for kitchen staff to track orders.
Cafe Gharti is a good setting for a prototype because a simple digital order
flow can reduce waiting and make order status easier to see.

## Problem statement

Cafe Gharti currently handles customer orders at the counter and tracks their
progress through informal communication between the counter and kitchen staff.
This causes long waits and uncertainty for customers, and makes it harder for
staff to keep orders organized during busy periods. Our prototype will display
the menu, accept customer orders, and let kitchen staff move each order through
received, preparing, ready, and picked up so that customers get clearer status
updates and staff can work from one shared order list.

## Target users

### Maya, a morning customer

- **Role:** Customer who stops for coffee and breakfast before work
- **Main goals:** Choose an item quickly, place an accurate order, and know when
  it is ready
- **Frustrations today:** Waiting in line and having to ask whether an order is
  ready
- **Comfort with technology:** Comfortable using websites and mobile ordering
- **Quote:** “I want to order quickly and get back to work without guessing
  when my coffee is ready.”

### Luis, a kitchen staff member

- **Role:** Staff member who prepares and updates customer orders
- **Main goals:** See new orders clearly, work through them in priority order,
  and mark completed orders
- **Frustrations today:** Verbal requests can be missed, and staff may not know
  which orders are already being prepared
- **Comfort with technology:** Comfortable with a simple tablet or computer
  interface
- **Quote:** “I need one clear list that shows what is new, what I am making,
  and what is ready.”

## Prototype scope

### Must-have features

1. A menu page showing available items, descriptions, and prices
2. An order form for selecting menu items and entering a customer name
3. Order confirmation after an order is submitted
4. A kitchen board showing received, preparing, ready, and picked-up orders
5. Controls for staff to move an order to its next status
6. An order history view for recently completed orders
7. A simple menu administration view for adding or updating items

### Not in this prototype

- Online payments or storing credit-card information
- SMS, email, or push-notification reminders
- A native iOS or Android mobile app
- Loyalty points, coupons, or customer accounts
- Delivery routing or third-party delivery integration
- Inventory tracking and automatic ingredient purchasing

## Questions

### 1. Why is a “not in this prototype” list useful?

The list keeps the prototype focused on the most important ordering and kitchen
workflow instead of allowing the project to grow without limits. It also gives
the client a clear explanation of what is not being promised yet, which helps
set expectations and makes future features easier to prioritize.

### 2. Which persona matters most to the business owner, and why?

Luis, the kitchen staff member, matters most to the business owner for the
first prototype because the kitchen workflow determines whether orders are
prepared accurately and on time. Improving that workflow can reduce mistakes
and waiting, which directly affects both operating efficiency and customer
satisfaction.
